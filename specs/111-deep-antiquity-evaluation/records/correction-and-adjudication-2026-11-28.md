## (c) Q-I-3 adjudication note — auxiliary assumptions

The broad transmission hypothesis (H) does not entail each
registered prediction without auxiliary assumptions. The
assumptions each prediction in fact requires are named here
so the historical grades are read as grades of the
predictions under those assumptions, not as a refutation of
every remote-source model:

- **P1 (inheritance)** requires that a common source would
  leave a shared derived feature set detectable by the
  registered instruments after millennia of separate
  development. The six descriptive profile features have no
  registered detection model for common descent; failure to
  cluster under that geometry is not a calibrated test that
  an ancestral relationship is absent. P1's NOT SUPPORTED
  describes no demonstrated shared derived feature set.
- **P2 (discontinuity)** requires the fully-formed-arrival
  variant: transmission without local precursor trails.
  Local precursor trails and different immediate script
  donors bear on that variant; proximal derivations are to
  be described as such, not as proof of unrelated ultimate
  origins, since H as registered permits transmission
  followed by separate development, borrowing, or script
  replacement.
- **P3 (chronology)** requires that transmission would leave
  a securely dated anomalous ancestor in the cited record;
  its NOT SUPPORTED is limited to no demonstrated secure
  anomalous ancestor in that record.
- **P4 (residue)** requires retention assumptions beyond
  merely transmitting writing: that transmitted advanced
  knowledge would be retained and would appear in surviving
  material in detectable form. Its grade is read under the
  bounded meaning at (b).

The evaluation's section 7 decisive-find statement is
narrowed of record: a sealed, independently dated
pre-3400 BCE written artifact demonstrably ancestral to a
program script would establish the chronology and ancestry
claims attaching to that line's origin — an extension of
the attested chronology and support for P3 for that line.
It would not by itself identify a catastrophe, a lost source
civilization, its transmitters, or a common origin across
the three systems, and it would not by itself make the
broader transmission account the leading account. Lesser
finds can likewise alter parts of the evidentiary assessment
without establishing H; the statement that "incremental
finds move nothing" is not retained at its original scope.
