## 1. The hypothesis in its strongest coherent form

**H (deep-antiquity transmission).** Before the
earliest writing the standard chronology knows, a
literate source civilization existed. Around a
catastrophe horizon (flooding and burning on a scale
that ended that civilization), its knowledge holders
transmitted writing, or the knowledge behind the
program's three languages and scripts, to multiple
recipient peoples. The recipient traditions then
developed separately, which is why the three systems
(Indus, Eteocypriot/Cypriot syllabic, Meroitic) are
mutually unintelligible today while (on H) sharing a
common ultimate source.

H is stated so it can lose. A hypothesis that is
compatible with every possible observation grades
nothing; §2 therefore derives only the observations H
distinctively predicts — expected if H is true, not
expected under the standard account (independent or
locally-derived origins within the documented
chronology).

## 2. The four distinctive predictions

- **P1 — Cross-system inheritance.** The three scripts
  or their ancestors share sign forms, structural
  conventions, or numerical practices beyond what
  chance and independent invention predict, in a
  pattern consistent with common descent rather than
  contact.
- **P2 — Developmental discontinuity.** A script
  appears without local developmental precursors,
  fully formed, at its first attestation (as a
  transmitted system would; a locally grown system
  leaves a precursor trail).
- **P3 — Chronological anomaly.** Secure dating places
  a script or its direct ancestor materially earlier
  than the standard chronology allows.
- **P4 — Content residue.** The earliest texts encode
  knowledge (astronomical, mathematical, calendrical)
  demonstrably beyond the recipient culture's
  independent attainment at that date (as imparted
  knowledge would; locally grown writing begins with
  accounts, names, and dedications).

## 3. Grading rule

Each prediction is graded per script where the
evidence separates, and overall, on the scale
**SUPPORTED / NOT SUPPORTED / UNTESTABLE ON CURRENT
EVIDENCE**. NOT SUPPORTED means the observations the
prediction requires are absent where they should be
present, or observations contrary to it are present.
It does not mean the hypothesis is impossible, and no
record of this study uses it that way. UNTESTABLE
names the missing evidence and what acquiring it
would take (§5 of the evaluation record).

## 4. The two evidence legs

1. **The documentary leg** (evaluation record §§2–5):
   dated stratigraphy and first attestations, the
   documented derivations (Meroitic from Egyptian
   hieroglyphic and demotic forms; the Cypriot
   syllabary from Cypro-Minoan in the Linear A
   tradition; Indus antecedent marks in the Ravi and
   Kot Diji phases at Harappa), and the scholarly
   status — labeled as such — of claimed older writing
   (Vinča signs, the Dispilio tablet, Jiahu marks).
   Every factual claim carries a checkable citation.
2. **The distributional leg** (evaluation record §2.3):
   a measurement run inside this spec on the program's
   own data — structural profiles of the three systems
   from the Spec 105 custody token streams, against
   the program's deciphered control (Latin, the Spec
   107 donor text) and a matched synthetic null, under
   one declared feature set, with its power and
   comparability limits on its face. P1's paleographic
   (sign-shape) leg is not measurable on program data:
   the program holds no sign-shape dataset for the
   three systems in comparable form, and §5 of the
   evaluation record states exactly that and what
   acquiring it would take.

## 5. What this study does not do

It does not date any artifact, re-excavate any
stratigraphy, or re-derive any script's origin from
primary material; the documentary leg grades the
predictions against the published scholarly record,
cited claim by claim. It does not move any standing,
verdict, anchor, or tally in any program, and its
grades are evaluation outcomes about predictions, not
adjudications of any claim in the claims ledger's
decipherment programs. It does not grade H as a whole
true or false: it grades its four predictions, and
closes with what H explains that the standard account
does not, what it costs in unsupported assumptions,
and the single new piece of evidence that would most
change the grading (evaluation record §6).

## 6. Kill criteria

1. A grade entered without its decisive evidence
   cited, or a citation that does not carry the claim
   it is attached to, voids the affected grade until
   repaired.
2. A factual claim in the evaluation record found
   misstated against its source is corrected by a
   dated amendment to the record, never silently.
3. If the distributional measurement's filed limits
   (corpus comparability, control count) are found to
   have been understated, the measurement is withdrawn
   as a leg and P1's grade rests on the documentary
   leg alone, with the withdrawal filed.
