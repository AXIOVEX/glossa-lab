## 2. The transcription: dedication A, syllabic side

The line stands about 12 mm above the alphabetic line;
its direction is normally sinistroverse, and it is
impossible, given the stone's state, to say where the
Cypriot part began at the right; there was probably
nothing above the end of ΑΦΡΟΔΙΤΗΙ (p. 238). The editors
identify "plus ou moins une quinzaine de signes", lightly
engraved, 8 to 12 mm high. Their sign-by-sign apparatus
(pp. 238–239), in their reading order:

| 2 | ko | "En forme de lambda majuscule, élargi en haut, donc ko" | identified by form |

| 4 | ? | "Très difficile, méconnaissable" | unread |

The editors' summary reading (p. 239): **"On lira
donc : [- - -]-ro-ko-ra-?-a-to-ro-ke-le-we-se-e-ro-ko-ro-
[- - -]."** The text is lost before sign 1 and after
sign 15 ("Ensuite, l'inscription est perdue").

Segmentation, on the editors' own groupings: signs 5–11
are the royal name, *a-to-ro-ke-le-we-se*, one word — a
first attestation of the name of King Androklès in a
non-contracted form corresponding to Ἀνδροκλῆς, in an
Eteocypriot case form not necessarily identical to the
Greek (pp. 238–239); signs 12–15, *e-ro-ko-ro*, are a
word or word-beginning, and the ICS 195 parallel (line
2, opening) already treated that sequence as an
independent word (p. 239); signs 1–4, *ro-ko-ra-?*, are
the residue preceding the name, grouped as the edition
prints them (p. 238: "On a donc ici une séquence
ro-ko-ra-?"). The editors' own bound stands on the
intake: "La présence du nom royal est ici le seul
élément assuré" (p. 239), and no hypothesis is offered
by them, or carried here, on the value of *e-ro-ko-ro*.

The same pass read the Greek side as printed (p. 239,
section B): Β[ασιλεὺς Ἀνδροκλῆς - - ca. 27 - -
Ὀρ]εσθέος καὶ Ἀνδραγόρου Κυπρίαι Ἀφροδίτηι, the
restorations being the editors'. It is carried in the
new corpus state as the edition's text, beside the
names and paraphrase already held.

## 4. Dedication B: no transcription exists to intake

Dedication B (IA 63, the digraph of 1979; first
published Hermary & Hellmann 1980, BCH 104, p. 262)
is returned to by the 1982 article at pp. 239–241 —
for its Greek text only. Of its syllabic part the
editors state (p. 239) that it "a presque
complètement disparu" — has almost completely
disappeared; Masson had proposed, with reserve, a
Greek legend for it, and the 1982 discussion
supersedes that in favour of Eteocypriot without
reading a single sign. A full read of the article
(pp. 235–244) confirms there is no sign-level
syllabic transcription of B anywhere in it. The
intake outcome for B is therefore not an extraction
failure but a property of the edition of record:
nothing exists there to transcribe. Its
`eteocypriot_side` remains null in the new corpus
state, with the side-status note rewritten to state
this ground.
