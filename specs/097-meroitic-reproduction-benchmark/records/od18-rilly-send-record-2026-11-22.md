---

Professor Rilly,

My name is Tristen Pierson. I run a small independent research program (Glossa-Lab, under Axiovex Systems) that works on undeciphered scripts with preregistered tests and published verdicts, including when the verdict goes against us.

This year we reproduced a published computational study of Meroitic and then ran a decipherment campaign against a frozen inventory of anchor readings drawn from the published tradition, mostly your work and Rilly and de Voogt (2012). Before scoring candidates we sent the inventory to Professor de Voogt and asked him to confirm or challenge it. He replied on November 20 and was clear that he could not confirm the translations, because the readings were established by Griffith and others and the wordlist was yours, and he said you were the only person who could properly answer. So the question comes to you.

The questions are short, and each one takes a confirm, a challenge with cause, or a no position:

1. Our scoring uses glosses from the published tradition, and your 2016 survey counts 39 lexemes as confirmed in meaning. Is it right to treat those 39 as confirmed for scoring purposes, and if not, which ones should come off that list?

2. Five glosses are used as scoring controls: qore "ruler, king", kdi "woman, lady", ste "mother", mk "god", and ato "water". Do you confirm each of the 5, or would you challenge any of them?

3. The inventory reads the funerary invocation as Isis (wosi) and Osiris (asiri, also written soreyi), and treats the filiation forms as fixing the mother relation (tede, "born of") and the father relation (terike, "begotten of") while the segmentation of those forms stays debated. Is that a fair statement of where things stand?

4. The closing clause of the funerary formula is carried in the received reading as "he/she was good", and the inventory flags it as the formula's least pinned slot. Should it stay flagged that way, or is there a better current reading?

5. The inventory carries qes "Kush" as provisional and excludes it from scoring controls, because of the qes/qos variation. Is provisional the right status for it?

6. Since 2012 and 2016, has the standing of any item in the published wordlist changed in a way that should stop us using it as an anchor?

You would be credited by name in the program record and in any publication that uses the check, whether your answers agree with the program or not. If you do not reply, I will record silence as silence, never as assent. There is no deadline on this.

I have not set terms for this on my side. If you or your institution need an arrangement of any kind, tell me what works and I will meet it if I can.

Thanks,
Tristen
